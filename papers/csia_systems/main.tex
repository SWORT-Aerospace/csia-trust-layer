\title{Measured Self-Occlusion in a Deployed Cross-Sensor Diagnostic Layer:\\
When a Fault Destroys the Evidence That Would Name It}

\author{Andres Felipe Pena Avila\\
SWORT LLC, Bethesda, Maryland, United States}

\begin{abstract}
We report measurements of \emph{false-healthy exposure} on a deployed
cross-sensor diagnostic layer for small uncrewed aircraft: the time the system
spends asserting health while holding no evidence it is entitled to assert it
from. The layer maintains per-sensor trust coefficients over a physics-derived
graph and runs a gated naive-Bayes diagnosis over eight mutually exclusive
causes. A gated feature is skipped rather than down-weighted, and the
implementation has no state meaning ``not assessable'', so absence of
applicable evidence is emitted as health. Under sustained GNSS denial the
diagnosis reports \code{nominal} for the entire fault window and never names
\code{gps\_fault}, while the GPS trust coefficient reads 0.9767. The vehicle is
not blind: the failsafe reaches CAUTION through an independent data-freshness
disjunct and reconfigures navigation. It cannot name the cause.

\textbf{Two scope corrections, both forced by our own controlled-injection
campaign against a real autopilot, are reported here rather than buried.}
First, this behaviour is specific to faults that remove the GNSS fix. A
position glitch that leaves the fix valid does not close the gate at all, so
the evidence remains available. Second, in that case the layer sees the fault
and still does not escalate: its residual detector saturates at the floor for
every injected position error we tested, from 5.6\,m to 5.6\,km, and no step
magnitude produces an alarm. The same total error applied as a ramp does.
Escalation here is duration-limited, not magnitude-limited.

We make no observability claim. Receiver observables detect GNSS denial well;
the failure we measure is one of evidence routing and applicability semantics,
not of detectability. We report a threshold result that yields a checkable
design rule, a chatter-resonance prediction tested against the deployed code
and holding, a one-line repair with its full measured cost, and a counterfactual
abstaining policy measured against current behaviour on the same flights. We
claim engineering contribution and measurement only.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Model-based diagnosis on a real vehicle rarely evaluates every residual at
every instant. Residuals are gated: a cross-check between barometric and GNSS
altitude is meaningless without a GNSS fix, and a thrust-versus-current
relation is meaningless before the motors are armed. Gating is correct
engineering. It is also where a specific failure mode lives.

The failure mode is this. If a fault closes a gate, and the features that gate
controls are the only evidence for that fault, then the fault removes its own
evidence. Nothing in the diagnosis reports an anomaly, because the anomalous
feature is not evaluated at all. The system does not become uncertain. It
becomes confident, and wrong, in the direction of health.

We measured it. On the deployed layer, with a GPS denial fault active for
40\,s, the diagnosis reported \code{nominal} on 100\% of post-onset ticks and
reached \code{gps\_fault} zero times. The GPS trust coefficient over the same
window never fell below 0.9767, because a channel that stops delivering data
has its belief update frozen rather than driven down: absence of evidence is
not treated as evidence of fault. Both the attribution channel and the trust
channel independently reported health while a fault the layer was built to
catch was active.

\textbf{The vehicle is not blind to the condition, and we state that before
any reader can form a wrong impression.} The failsafe policy escalates to
CAUTION and issues \code{SWITCH\_NAV\_INERTIAL} and \code{INHIBIT\_GPS\_MODES}
in this scenario, driven by a data-freshness disjunct that does not consult the
diagnosis. The gap is in attribution, not in escalation. What is lost is the
ability to name the cause, and therefore the ability to choose a
cause-specific response, to log a correct incident record, or to distinguish
GNSS denial from a benign loss of reception.

\textbf{The condition is narrower than the failure mode described above, and
we bound it here rather than in the limitations.} A controlled-injection
campaign against a full ArduPilot autopilot
(Section~\ref{sec:qgvalid}) finds that faults which remove the GNSS fix close
the gate almost always, and that a 62 to 76\,m position glitch which keeps the
fix does not close it at all. Self-occlusion is a property of fix-loss faults,
not of GNSS faults in general.

\textbf{We make no claim that GNSS denial is hard to observe, because it is
not.} Receiver-level observables detect it: carrier-to-noise density, automatic
gain control behaviour, loss of lock, satellite count, correlation-peak
distortion and RAIM status are all established means, and none of them is
exotic. The failure we report is that this system's diagnosis is routed
entirely through position-domain relations that its own validity gate switches
off, and that it has no vocabulary for ``I cannot assess this''. It is an
evidence-routing and applicability-semantics failure, and Section~\ref{sec:repair}
prices the fix at one ungated relation.

We make no mathematical claim. Diagnosis under intermittent and permanent loss
of observations is established
\cite{carvalho2012intermittent,carvalho2013permanent}, as is diagnosability
under state-dependent observation \cite{yin_phidiagnosability} and, for the
fault-state-dependent case that ours is an instance of,
\cite{dong2024statedependent}. Structural isolability with mode-dependent
signatures is established
\cite{krysander2024multimode,krysander2008sensorplacement}, and the
reliability-side machinery we apply is likewise established
\cite{hurdle2007faulttree,reeves2019sensorselection}.

\paragraph{Contribution.} We studied which GNSS fault classes a deployed
cross-sensor diagnostic layer detects, which it escalates, and how long it
reports health without applicable evidence, using controlled injection against
a real autopilot; we report the fault classes where it and the vehicle's own
estimator are both blind, a closed-form account of why detection does not
imply escalation, and the fault-injection harness we used.

\paragraph{Fault taxonomy.} Every result below is reported against these four
classes, distinguished by what each does to \emph{applicability}: whether the
test for \code{gps\_fault} can be evaluated at all.

\section{Related work: what we instantiate}
\label{sec:related}

We list, for each framework, what we take from it and what we do not claim.

\paragraph{Diagnosis under unreliable observation.}
Carvalho et al. treat diagnosability when observations are lost intermittently
\cite{carvalho2012intermittent} and permanently
\cite{carvalho2013permanent}, including the case where the diagnoser is
unaware that a sensor has failed. \emph{What we take}: the framing that
observation availability is part of the diagnosis problem rather than a
precondition of it. \emph{What we claim}: nothing. Our gate closure is an
instance of observation loss in their sense.

\paragraph{State-dependent observation maps.}
Yin et al.\ develop diagnosability when the observation map depends on system
state \cite{yin_phidiagnosability}. \emph{What we take}: the recognition that
a hypothesis-dependent observation map is a studied object.
\emph{What we claim}: nothing.

\paragraph{Fault-state-dependent observation loss.}
Dong, Yin and Li verify diagnosability under a state-dependent observation map
$O: Q \times \Sigma \to 2^{\Delta \cup \{\varepsilon\}}$, which permits the
observation available at a transition to depend on the state the system is in,
including a fault state \cite{dong2024statedependent}. Our situation maps onto
theirs directly: a fault-labelled augmented state corresponds to the hypothesis
being active, $O(q,\sigma) = \{\varepsilon\}$ corresponds to gate closure
silencing a feature, and failure of $\varphi$-diagnosability corresponds to
what we call effective undetectability. \emph{What we take}: the general
mechanism of fault-induced observation suppression, entire.
\emph{What we claim}: nothing. Priority for general fault-induced suppression
belongs to them. What is left to us is a measurement on a deployed system and
a scoped enumeration of one system's evidence set, neither of which is a
statement about the general mechanism.

\paragraph{Structural isolability and mode-dependent signatures.}
Krysander and colleagues compute isolability from structural models and select
residuals that preserve diagnosability across operating modes
\cite{krysander2024multimode}, and compute minimal sensor sets for an
isolability specification \cite{krysander2008sensorplacement}. \emph{What we
take}: the design-time signature matrix and its induced partition, which we
use as the baseline our measurement is compared against. \emph{What we claim}:
nothing. Our design-time analysis is theirs.

\paragraph{Fault trees and sensor selection by diagnostic performance.}
Hurdle et al.\ apply fault-tree methods to diagnosing system operation
\cite{hurdle2007faulttree}; Reeves et al.\ select sensors against a diagnostic
performance metric \cite{reeves2019sensorselection}. \emph{What we take}: the
whole apparatus of Section~\ref{sec:repair}'s cut-set analysis, unchanged.
\emph{What we claim}: nothing. Applying coherent-system structure functions to
diagnostic capability is their contribution, not ours.

\paragraph{Isolation to the granularity of reconfiguration.}
Blanke et al.\ establish that faults need only be distinguished to the
granularity that the reconfiguration map requires \cite{blanke2003ftc}. We use
this to define the decision-relevant pair set in
Section~\ref{sec:system}. \emph{What we claim}: nothing.

\section{System under measurement}
\label{sec:system}

The layer maintains a trust coefficient $T_i \in (0,1)$ per sensor node over a
graph of 8 nodes and 11 physics-derived edges. Each edge and each per-node
physics self-model produces a chi-square-normalised residual $\lambda$, mapped
to a consistency score
\begin{equation}
c = \exp\!\left(-\tfrac{1}{2}\max(0, \lambda - 1)\right),
\end{equation}
so that only excess over the chi-square nominal penalises trust. Fused
evidence updates a Beta belief with adaptive forgetting $\rho \in [0.95,
0.998]$, and the belief update is skipped entirely when a channel's data is
stale.

Diagnosis is a gated naive Bayes over 8 mutually exclusive hypotheses. Each
feature $f$ carries a gate $g_f \in [0,1]$, and the accumulation is
\begin{equation}
\ell_h \mathrel{+}= g_f \cdot
\begin{cases}
\log(1 - c_f + \varepsilon) & f \in \mathrm{LOW}(h)\\
\log(c_f + \varepsilon) & \text{otherwise,}
\end{cases}
\qquad \varepsilon = 0.02,
\end{equation}
followed by exponential smoothing with $\alpha_s = 0.06$, tempering by 1.5,
and a softmax against a prior placing 0.86 on \code{nominal}.

\textbf{We call the output a normalized hypothesis weight, not a posterior.}
The three operations just listed each break the Bayesian reading, and any one
of them alone would suffice: the evidence term is an exponential moving average
over roughly fifteen ticks rather than the current tick's likelihood, it is
raised to the power 1.5 before normalization, and $\varepsilon = 0.02$ is a
floor inside the logarithm rather than a density. The weights sum to one and
rank the hypotheses, which is what the assurance layer uses them for, but they
are not $P(h \mid \text{observation at tick } k)$ and we do not report them as
probabilities. Both the batch and the real-time paths compute the same
quantity by the same three steps.

\textbf{The load-bearing implementation detail} is that a feature with
$g_f \le 10^{-9}$ is skipped by a \code{continue}, not down-weighted. It
therefore contributes to no hypothesis. Two hypotheses whose LOW sets coincide
over the surviving features accumulate identical likelihood indefinitely, and
the ratio of their normalized hypothesis weights remains pinned at the
prior ratio. This is
indistinguishability, not weak evidence.

\subsection{Design-time and effective signatures}

Let $S[f,h]=1$ when hypothesis $h$ perturbs feature $f$. Write
$\mathrm{closes}(h,g)$ when $h$ drives gate $g$ closed, and define
\begin{equation}
S_{\mathrm{eff}}[f,h] = S[f,h] \wedge
\neg\big(\exists\, g \in \mathrm{gate}(f) : \mathrm{closes}(h,g)\big).
\label{eq:seff}
\end{equation}
A feature counts as evidence for $h$ only if $h$ perturbs it and does not
silence it. Since $S_{\mathrm{eff}} \le S$ entrywise, the induced partition
$\Pi(S_{\mathrm{eff}})$ is no finer than $\Pi(S)$. This is monotonicity of
distinguishability under deletion of observations and we do not present it as
more than that.

Applied to this system, with $\kappa = (H-P)/(H-1)$ for $H=8$ hypotheses and
$P$ observable classes:
$\kappa(S) = 0.000$ over 8 classes, and
$\kappa(S_{\mathrm{eff}}) = 0.143$ over 7 classes, the merged pair being
\code{gps\_fault} and \code{nominal}. The design-time artifact reports full
isolability for a system that does not have it.

\subsection{Decision-relevant pairs}

Following \cite{blanke2003ftc}, only distinctions that change the commanded

\section{Containment: what is established and what is not}
\label{sec:containment}

Two conditions must be kept apart. Write $E_h$ for the features hypothesis $h$
actually references in the deployed signature matrix, $U_h$ for the set of all
physically admissible relations that could bear on $h$, and $C_h$ for the set
of features controlled by gates that $h$ itself closes.

\begin{align}
E_h &\subseteq C_h && \text{present support silenced} \label{eq:eh}\\
U_h &\subseteq C_h && \text{whole universe silenced} \label{eq:uh}
\end{align}

Condition~\eqref{eq:eh} gives effective undetectability now, for the system as
built. Condition~\eqref{eq:uh} would give irreparability: no addition of
evidence could ever repair the collapse. \textbf{We establish
\eqref{eq:eh} and we do not establish \eqref{eq:uh}.} An earlier draft of this
work did not separate them.

\paragraph{\eqref{eq:eh} holds for \code{gps\_fault}, by inspection.}
$E_{\code{gps\_fault}} = \{\code{phys\_gps}\}$, read from the signature matrix
in \code{bayes.py}. \code{phys\_gps} is gated by
$\code{gvalid} \cdot \mathbb{1}[\tau > 3]$, read from \code{online.py}, so
$\code{phys\_gps} \in C_{\code{gps\_fault}}$. A one-element subset check is the
whole proof. This is what Section~\ref{sec:measurement} measures the
consequence of.

\paragraph{\eqref{eq:uh} is not established, and we drop it permanently.}
$U_h$ has no absolute definition. It is definable only relative to a triple
$(M, S, R)$: a measurement set $M$ the platform actually provides, a signal
model $S$ that says what those measurements mean, and a relation language $R$
that says what may be built from them. Change any of the three and $U_h$
changes. There is therefore no unrestricted irreparability claim to be made,
and we withdraw the one an earlier draft of this work made. What remains is
containment relative to a stated $(M, S, R)$, which is what
Section~\ref{sec:fieldset} reports.

\paragraph{Containment is cheap to check, and we do not claim otherwise.}
Deciding $E_h \subseteq C_h$ is a subset test over two finite sets, linear in
the size of the signature matrix and the gate map. The design-rule audit of
Section~\ref{sec:rule} runs it over all 19 signature entries in the project's
test suite on every commit. Nothing here is computationally hard, and any
framing that suggests otherwise would be wrong.

\subsection{A generator-dependent sensitivity study}

this system averages 2.4 features per hypothesis, and \code{gps\_fault} has
exactly one. If the collapse disappears once hypotheses carry more evidence,
the finding is about sparsity and not about gating.

We tested this by synthesising signature matrices over the same gate structure
at densities from 1 to 8 features per hypothesis, 400 draws per cell, with one
designated hypothesis closing \code{gvalid}. Two arms differ only in where
that hypothesis draws its features from.

\subsection{The interior is a threshold, not a slope}
\label{sec:knifeedge}

Arms A and B are corners. We swept the interior: let a fraction $\rho$ of the
designated hypothesis's features lie inside $C_h$ and $1-\rho$ outside, at
densities 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 and 8, 400 draws per cell.

The result is a threshold at $\rho = 1$. With every feature inside $C_h$ the
hypothesis is absorbed into the nominal class in 100\% of draws; with
\emph{exactly one} feature outside, it is absorbed in 0\% of draws. The drop is
1.000 at every density tested. Effective undetectability does not degrade
gracefully with partial containment; it requires full containment of the
present support.

The engineering statement this licenses is narrower and more useful than
anything about universes: \textbf{one ungated relation suffices}. That is a
checkable design requirement, and Section~\ref{sec:repair} costs it out.

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/wp_c6_gap_surface.png}
\caption{The interior of the containment sweep. Left: mean gap against
$\rho$. Right: probability the designated hypothesis is absorbed into the
nominal class. The transition at $\rho = 1$ is a step at every density.}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{A correction to our own reasoning.} An earlier note in this project
justified $\Pi(S_{\mathrm{eff}})$ being no finer than $\Pi(S)$ by observing
that $S_{\mathrm{eff}} \le S$ entrywise, so equal columns stay equal. That
justification is wrong in general. Entrywise domination preserves column
equality under \emph{uniform} row deletion, but per-hypothesis silencing is
\emph{column-dependent}: two hypotheses sharing a column in $S$ separate in
$S_{\mathrm{eff}}$ when only one of them silences, and the partition gets
finer. Searching 200\,000 random draws found 483 counterexamples, a rate of
0.24\%. The deployed matrix is unaffected, because \code{gps\_fault} is the
only hypothesis that silences anything and its column is unique, so no shared
column can be broken. The computational verification on the real matrix stands;
the general argument does not.

six are \code{gvalid}-gated.}
\label{tab:fieldset}
\end{table}

Formal containment over this field set is therefore \emph{false}: one relation
sits outside. But a relation outside $C_h$ is only an escape if its value
responds to the fault. Running the deployed pipeline clean and under denial on
the same truth and seed, \code{temp\_gps} consistency over the fault window is
bit-identical between the two runs (mean 0.9155, min 0.640319, max 1.0 in
both) while the GPS channel's fresh fraction falls from 1.0 to 0.001597. The
temporal predictor is computed from the channel history and never consults
channel validity, so a dropout leaves the underlying series and therefore the
residual untouched. \code{temp\_gps} is ungated and carries no information
about denial.

\textbf{The scoped statement we can defend:} over the currently ingested
telemetry field set, formal containment is incomplete by one relation and
\emph{effective} containment is complete. This is weaker than \eqref{eq:uh} and
much stronger than nothing, and it is defensible because the enumeration is
mechanical and the field set is closed.

It also names the escape route precisely. Every GPS-spanning relation
buildable from the ingested fields depends on position, velocity or altitude,
all of which are meaningless without a fix and are gvalid-gated by
construction. The fields that could support a relation surviving fix loss are
\code{satellites\_used}, \code{noise\_per\_ms}, \code{jamming\_indicator},
\code{hdop}, \code{vdop}, \code{s\_variance\_m\_s} and \code{eph}/\code{epv},
and the ingestion path reads none of them.

\section{Gate closure frequency, measured against a real autopilot}
\label{sec:qgvalid}

Everything above is structural: it says what can happen, not how often. The
quantity that converts it into a risk statement is
$q_{\mathrm{gvalid}} \mid h = P(\code{gvalid}\ \text{closed at a tick} \mid h)$,
and it cannot be estimated from the public log corpus because that corpus is
unannotated and the conditioning variable does not exist in it.

We obtained the label by controlled injection instead. A full ArduCopter
autopilot runs in software-in-the-loop with its own EKF, its own GPS driver
and its own MAVLink stream; the deployed trust layer ingests that stream
through the production adapter, and the gate trace is the layer's own
\code{gvalid} read out of its per-tick edge record rather than recomputed. Four
arms of six flights each share the same six flight profiles, so the design is
matched. Each flight arms, climbs to 20\,m, flies a square, and receives its
injection at $t = 60$\,s; the conditioning window is $[60, 85]$\,s.
Pre-registration, stopping rules and the estimator follow a specification
fixed before the campaign ran.

\paragraph{A correction to our own arm labels.} The fourth arm was originally
called \code{degraded} and described as partial constellation loss, because it
combined a reduced satellite count with receiver byte loss. A follow-up sweep
(Section~\ref{sec:ladder}) shows the satellite count contributes nothing: the
gate does not close at any satellite count. The closure came entirely from the
byte loss, which is a receiver link failure, so the arm is relabelled. The
number is unchanged; the attribution was wrong.

Three things follow, and the third is the one that changes the paper.

\paragraph{The gate never closed in healthy flight.} Zero closures in 3744
monitored ticks. By the pre-registered stopping rule a zero denominator makes
the ratio unbounded above, so we report the one-sided statement only and no
interval. This is also the weakest of the three results: the simulated receiver
does not spontaneously lose a fix, so a nominal rate of zero is a property of
the generator before it is a statement about flight.

\paragraph{Denial closes the gate, end to end, on a real autopilot.}
$q_{\mathrm{gvalid}} \mid \code{denial} = 0.984$, and the diagnosis over the
same window reports \code{nominal} at $p = 0.977$ with \code{gps\_fault} at
$p = 0.0227$, while the failsafe reaches CAUTION at $t = 61.5$\,s through the
freshness disjunct. That reproduces Section~\ref{sec:measurement} on a
different generator, with a different vehicle model, a different EKF and the
production ingestion adapter in the path. It is confirmation of the mechanism,
not evidence about frequency: \code{SIM\_GPS\_DISABLE} switches the fix flag
off, so a high closure rate under it is close to tautological.

\paragraph{A GNSS fault that keeps the fix does not close the gate.} This is
the informative arm. A position glitch of 62 to 76\,m, with ten satellites
still reported and the fix retained, produced \textbf{zero} gate closures in
3744 ticks. For that fault class the design-time and effective partitions
coincide, so the self-occlusion of Section~\ref{sec:measurement} does not
arise at all: the evidence stays available.

The layer did not use it. GPS trust fell only to 0.906--0.934 against a
\code{nav\_caution} threshold of 0.75, no failsafe transition occurred, and the
diagnosis stayed at \code{nominal} $p = 0.9997$. \textbf{That is a different
failure mode from the one this paper is about} and we do not merge them: the
first is evidence destroyed by gating, the second is evidence present and not
acted on. Section~\ref{sec:glitch} tests its cause.

\paragraph{What this bounds.} Self-occlusion is specific to fix-loss faults,
not to GNSS faults in general. Both arms that removed the fix closed the gate;
the arm that kept it did not. The scope of Section~\ref{sec:measurement} is
therefore narrower than an unqualified reading would suggest, and this campaign
is what narrows it.

\paragraph{Limits of this campaign, stated with the result.} The per-flight
statistic is identical across flights in every arm, so the bootstrap interval
collapses to a point. That interval is a statement about simulator determinism

narrow. The receiver is simulated throughout, and nothing here is a measurement
of real GNSS environments.

\subsection{What varied, checked rather than assumed}
\label{sec:variance}

Zero between-flight variance is not a normal experimental result, so before
building on the campaign we tested whether the varied parameters reached the
simulation at all. If they had not, the campaign would be one condition
replicated 24 times and every number above would be wrong.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Home site: real.} Six sites produced mean attitude differences
with a between-site standard deviation of 0.0127\,rad against a within-site
standard deviation of $5.9 \times 10^{-5}$\,rad, measured by flying one site
three times. The ratio is 216, so the sites are genuinely distinct conditions.
\item \textbf{Wind: cosmetic, and this corrects our own description.}
\code{SIM\_WIND\_SPD} is accepted and reads back correctly at every value, but
a vehicle commanded to hold position tilts $0.159^\circ$ at 0\,m/s and
$0.159^\circ$ at 12\,m/s. No wind force is applied in this build. The campaign
was described as varying two axes and varied one.
\item \textbf{Gate response: deterministic.} First closure occurs 0.44\,s after
injection in every denial flight. The injection dominates the gate regardless
of where the vehicle is, which is why the between-flight variance is zero. It
is a property of the gate response, not an artifact of the experiment.
\end{itemize}

The marginal value of additional flights follows from this directly: bootstrap
interval width is zero at every $n$ from 2 to 12, because there is no
between-flight variance to resample. Flying more of the same condition buys
nothing. The binding constraint is condition diversity, which is what the rest
of this section spends its flights on.

\subsection{The satellite ladder, and a fault the layer cannot see at all}
\label{sec:ladder}

\code{SIM\_GPS\_NUMSATS} was swept from 9 down to 0, one flight per rung.
\textbf{The gate never closed.} At every rung, including zero satellites, the
receiver continued to report a 3D fix on all 578 samples and
$q_{\mathrm{gvalid}} = 0.000$, while \code{satellites\_visible} tracked the
commanded value exactly.

Two readings, and both belong in the record.

The first is a fidelity limit: a real receiver does not assert a 3D fix on zero
satellites, so this simulator's fix flag is not coupled to its own satellite
count, and the ladder does not tell us where a real receiver would flip.

The second is the paper's point demonstrated end to end. The injected fault was
fully present in the telemetry, in a field the receiver was reporting
correctly, and the layer never saw it: \code{satellites\_used} is not among the
fields the ingestion path reads, and the gate is keyed on fix validity alone.
The diagnosis reported \code{nominal} throughout. Nothing was unobservable;
the observation was not routed.

\section{The glitch non-alarm has a tested cause}
\label{sec:glitch}

The \code{glitch} arm leaves the fix valid, so the gate stays open and the
evidence stays available. The layer did not escalate anyway. We tested why.

\paragraph{The hypothesis we started from, and its fate.} The natural
explanation is that the EKF absorbed the step into its state, the innovation
stayed small, and a residual monitor saw nothing. That is why RAIM operates in
the measurement domain rather than the position domain. It splits into two
claims and they do not have the same answer. The EKF \emph{did} absorb the
step: its fused position ends up following the glitched GNSS position by a
fraction of 0.79 to 0.998 across the sweep. Its innovation did \emph{not} stay
small: reported horizontal position variance rises from about 0.006 to between
1.2 and 1113, scaling with glitch size. And neither half would have explained
the non-alarm, because this layer reads the raw receiver output and never
consumes the fused estimate.

\paragraph{What the sweep shows instead.} Step glitches from 5.6\,m to 5.6\,km:

The detector is not blind. It saturates at zero for every injected error,
including 5.6\,m, so the layer sees every glitch. What bounds the trust
excursion is not magnitude but \emph{duration}: the step detector decays at
0.85 per tick, so a step floors the residual for 26 to 40 ticks whatever its
size, and the trust recursion integrates over a window long enough that this
never crosses the threshold. Trust saturates near 0.906 from 55\,m upward.

\textbf{There is no step magnitude at which this system alarms.} A GNSS
position error of five kilometres, arriving as a step, is seen by the detector
and never escalated.

\paragraph{And a ramp inverts the ordering.} The same total 222.6\,m offset
applied as a 5, 20 or 60\,s ramp does alarm, with minimum trust 0.717 to 0.723
and 104 to 168 ticks at the floor. A slow error holds the residual above
threshold long enough to move the recursion; a fast one of the same size does
not. This is the opposite of what one would expect from an estimator-tracking
argument, and it is the opposite of the EKF's own behaviour: the 20 and 60\,s
ramps are absorbed completely, with EKF variance staying near baseline. Each
monitor is blind to what the other sees.

\subsection{Why, in closed form}
\label{sec:algebra}

The trust coefficient is a Beta belief with adaptive forgetting
(\code{engine.py}). Two facts fix the behaviour.

First, the belief mass $S_k = \alpha_k + \beta_k$ obeys
$S_{k+1} = \rho S_k + 1$ regardless of the evidence, so it converges to
$1/(1-\rho)$. Calm flight has zero spread between evidence components and runs
at $\rho_{\max} = 0.998$, so the belief settles carrying
$S_0 = 1/(1-\rho_{\max}) = 500$ units of accumulated agreement.

Second, when a fault destroys one evidence component the spread jumps to 1 and
$\rho$ drops to its floor $\rho_{\min} = 0.95$, whose sustainable mass is only
$M = 20$. Holding evidence at a constant $e_f$ for $N$ ticks then gives, with
$x = \rho^N$,

\begin{equation}
T_N = \frac{x S_0 T_0 + (1-x) M e_f}{x S_0 + (1-x) M}
\label{eq:trustlag}
\end{equation}

a mass-weighted average of the old trust and the new evidence. Two corollaries
follow, and both are measured above.

\paragraph{The evidence floor.} At the GPS node the fusion weights are
$w_{nbr}, w_{phys}, w_{temp}, w_{stat} = 0.35, 0.30, 0.20, 0.15$. A fault that
destroys only the physics residual leaves
$e_f = 0.35 + 0.20 + 0.15 = 0.70$, against a threshold of 0.75. Trust cannot
fall below 0.70 however large the error. Measured minimum fused evidence in the
saturated cells of the sweep: \textbf{0.7000}.

\paragraph{The ticks required.} Setting $T_N = T^*$ in \eqref{eq:trustlag},

\begin{equation}
N^* = \frac{\ln \left( \frac{M(T^* - e_f)}{S_0(T_0 - T^*) + M(T^* - e_f)}
\right)}{\ln \rho} \approx 93 \text{ ticks} = 3.7\,\text{s}
\label{eq:nstar}
\end{equation}

Note which term dominates: $S_0(T_0 - T^*) = 118.82$ against
$M(T^* - e_f) = 1.0$. \textbf{The delay is set by how much agreement the calm
period banked, not by how bad the fault is.} Halving the concentration cap
nearly halves the delay; doubling the threshold margin barely moves it.

Escalation additionally requires the failsafe's $1\,\text{s}$ dwell, so the
boundary is $N^* + 25 \approx 118$ ticks at the floor. Against the 21 cells of
the sweep this classifies \textbf{19 correctly}. Both misses lie near the line,
at $+0.7$ and $-12.3$ ticks, and the model assumes a constant $e_f$ where the
measured floor ranges 0.678 to 0.700.

Equation~\eqref{eq:trustlag} was checked against the shipping recursion driven
with a synthetic evidence sequence: maximum absolute error \textbf{0.00} over
200 ticks, and the predicted crossing at 93.3 ticks against an observed
crossing at tick 94.

\paragraph{And why magnitude cannot substitute for duration.} The step detector
holds a peak decaying at $0.85$ per tick, so the residual stays saturated for
$N_{floor} \propto \ln(\text{amplitude})$ ticks. A thousandfold larger error
buys at most 42 extra ticks, every one of them on a residual already pinned at
zero and therefore contributing no further evidence. \textbf{A saturating
detector with a fixed decay cannot escalate on magnitude alone.}

boundary. Right: how each axis buys time.}

barometric predictions. They read different signals, so their blind spots need
not coincide. We swept amplitude against slew rate, 21 cells, recording for

\textbf{The uncovered region is not empty and it is not a corner.} It is a

build and one diagnostic layer, and we make no claim about detectors in

\section{False-healthy exposure, and the cost of abstaining}
\label{sec:falsehealthy}

The quantity that matters operationally is not whether a gate closes but how
long the system asserts health while holding nothing it is entitled to assert
it from, and for how much of that time it is actually wrong. Those are two
different questions and Section~\ref{sec:fourq} separates them into four.

\subsection{The four states, and what this system emits instead}

The implementation has no \code{NOT\_APPLICABLE} state. Reading what it emits
in each case, from source, is the finding.

\centering
\small
\begin{tabular}{p{0.20\textwidth}p{0.40\textwidth}p{0.30\textwidth}}
\toprule
State & Condition & What is emitted \\
\midrule

Two lines of source produce the last two rows. In the diagnosis, a gated
feature is skipped outright, so when a hypothesis's entire support is gated its
likelihood ties with \code{nominal} over the surviving features and the prior
decides; the normalized hypothesis weight concentrates on health. In the trust recursion, a node
that loses every evidence source has its Beta belief held rather than driven
down, which is the right choice given the available vocabulary. A held belief
and a confirmed one are the same number at the output.

This is a semantics gap, not an observability limit. The evidence for GNSS
denial exists at the receiver and is well studied; this system routes its
diagnosis through position-domain relations that the fault switches off, and
then has no way to say so.

\subsection{Four quantities, because one was not enough}
\label{sec:fourq}

A single exposure number conflated two different things. Time spent healthy
with no applicable evidence is an \emph{unsupported} verdict; without ground
truth it may still be correct by accident, and calling it false overclaims.
Controlled injection supplies the ground truth an operational log cannot, so
the quantity splits. Let $A_h = 1$ mean the test for $h$ is applicable at this
tick and $F_h = 1$ mean the injection schedule says $h$ is truly present:

\begin{align*}
T_{NA}   &= \text{time}\{ A_h = 0 \} & &\text{inapplicable}\\
T_{UH}   &= \text{time}\{ A_h = 0 \wedge \text{healthy} \} &
  &\text{unsupported healthy}\\
T_{FH}   &= \text{time}\{ F_h = 1 \wedge \text{healthy} \} &
  &\text{false healthy}\\
T_{SOFH} &= \text{time}\{ F_h = 1 \wedge A_h = 0 \wedge \text{healthy} \} &
  &\text{self-occluded false healthy}
\end{align*}

Only $T_{SOFH}$ is this paper's original subject. Verdicts are reported in the
ISO/IEC 9646-1 conformance vocabulary, PASS, FAIL, INCONCLUSIVE and
INAPPLICABLE, presented as HEALTHY, FAULT, UNKNOWN and NOT\_APPLICABLE. That
unusable data must not be presented as normal data is established at field
level: ARINC 429 carries a Sign/Status Matrix distinguishing Failure Warning
and No Computed Data from Normal Operation, and OPC UA carries a StatusCode
whose Bad severity means the value must not be used. We are aware of no
standard term for the exposure quantity itself and do not coin one.

\subsection{The abstaining baseline}

We implemented, \emph{in research code only and with no production change}, a
counterfactual policy that emits \code{NOT\_APPLICABLE} when a hypothesis's
entire support is silenced. Scored on the same flights:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{It removes the self-occlusion exposure completely.}
$T_{SOFH}$ falls to 0.00\,s in every arm, and $T_{FH}$ falls from 24.37 to
0.40\,s under denial and from 24.96 to 0.48\,s under link loss.
\item \textbf{It does nothing for the glitch.} $T_{FH}$ stays at 24.64\,s,
unchanged to two decimals. There is nothing to abstain about when $A_h = 1$
throughout. \emph{The two defects need two different fixes, and this is the
measurement that shows it.}
\item Onset of a non-healthy status moves from 1.48\,s to \textbf{0.44\,s}
under denial and 1.56\,s to 0.52\,s under link loss. The abstention is
available as soon as the gate closes, without waiting for the freshness
watchdog.
\item \textbf{Nuisance cost is zero.} In \code{nominal} the policy never
fires: zero silenced ticks, zero abstentions, behaviour identical to the
deployed system.
\item \textbf{Recovery costs nothing.} After denial ends while airborne, both
policies return to health after 5.71\,s, the failsafe's own de-escalation
dwell.
\end{itemize}

On this evidence the abstaining state is one-sided for the faults it addresses,
and irrelevant to the faults it does not. We report it as a measured
counterfactual and not as a recommendation to ship, because three flights per
arm against one simulator is not the basis for a production change.

\section{Chatter resonance}
\label{sec:chatter}

Exponential smoothing with coefficient $\alpha_s$ has a characteristic time
constant $1/\alpha_s$. A gate that toggles on that timescale should maximise
label instability: faster toggling is averaged out, slower toggling lets the
accumulator settle in each state. With $\alpha_s = 0.06$ the predicted peak is
at $1/\alpha_s = 16.67$ frames.

We swept gate closure duration from 1 to 100 frames against the deployed
diagnosis kernel, holding the evidence pattern fixed so that every label
change is caused by availability alone. The observed peak is at 21 frames with
95 top-label changes per 2000 frames, against 1 to 3 changes at durations
below 18. The prediction holds on the implementation.

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.85\textwidth]{figures/wp_c4_chatter_resonance.png}
\caption{Top-label changes against gate closure duration. Evidence held fixed,
so every change is caused by gating.}
\end{figure}

A methodological note. Our first attempt at this sweep made every feature
anomalous, which allowed a multi-feature hypothesis to lead whether the gate
was open or closed, and produced zero label changes at every duration. That
null result was a property of the probe, not of the system. The gated feature
must be decisive for the leading label or there is nothing to observe.

\section{A design rule and its enforcement}
\label{sec:rule}

\textbf{One ungated relation suffices.} That is the transferable claim, and it
is the threshold result of Section~\ref{sec:knifeedge} restated as
engineering. Absorption of the hypothesis into the nominal class runs at 1.000
when every one of its features lies inside $C_h$ and at 0.000 when exactly one
lies outside, at every signature density from 1 to 8. There is no dose-response
curve to tune against and no partial credit: the requirement is a single
relation whose gate is independent of the fault, and the requirement is met or
it is not.

That sharpness is what makes the rule checkable rather than advisory:

\begin{quote}
A feature intended to discriminate hypothesis $h$ must not be gated by a gate
that $h$ closes.
\end{quote}

Auditing this system's 19 signature entries gives 18 clean and one violation:
\code{gps\_fault} / \code{phys\_gps} / \code{gvalid}. The violation is fatal
rather than merely weakening because \code{phys\_gps} is that hypothesis's sole
feature, so the effective signature empties rather than thins.

The rule is enforced in the project's test suite. The test pins the violation
list exactly: a new violation fails the build, and the known violation
disappearing also fails, so a silent repair cannot land without the record
being updated in the same commit. This is the transferable engineering output
of the work. It requires only a signature matrix and a gate-to-feature map,
both of which any gated diagnostic system already has.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

\paragraph{The corpus is unannotated.} The 26 public flight logs used
elsewhere in this project carry no ground-truth health labels. No detection
performance on real flight data is claimed anywhere in this paper. The
measurements in Sections~\ref{sec:measurement} and \ref{sec:chatter} are on
simulated fault injection into the deployed pipeline.

\paragraph{Closure attribution is derived, not observed.}
$\mathrm{closes}(h,g)$ comes from reading the gate predicate in source plus the
physical fault model. If a fault closes a gate in flight that the model says it
does not, Equation~\ref{eq:seff} is wrong in the optimistic direction and the
real gap is larger than reported.

\paragraph{Structural, not statistical.} $\kappa$ bounds structural
indistinguishability. Two hypotheses in one observable class may still separate
on residual magnitude or variance. In arm B they did not, but that is one
scenario and not a proof.

\paragraph{The one frequency we measure is measured in a simulator.}
Section~\ref{sec:qgvalid} is the only frequency in this paper, and its
generator is ArduPilot SITL. No closure rate on real flight data is claimed
anywhere. The public corpus cannot supply one: it is unannotated, so the
conditioning variable does not exist in it, and reporting an unconditional
closure rate as a substitute would mix hypotheses in unknown proportion and
bound nothing. We have specified the real-telemetry measurement separately and
have not performed it.

\paragraph{The governing objection, stated in full.} The strongest objection to
Section~\ref{sec:containment} is this one, and we reproduce it as it was put to
us:

\begin{quote}
``Unless the admissible evidence universe is defined independently, shown
complete, and difficult to compute, this is a modeling observation rather than
a new theorem.''
\end{quote}

We do not defeat it, and we accept all three parts. $U_h$ is not defined
independently: it is definable only relative to $(M, S, R)$, as
Section~\ref{sec:containment} now states. It is not shown complete except over
a field set we enumerate and name. And it is not difficult to compute; the
containment test is a linear-time subset check that runs in our test suite on
every commit, and we say so affirmatively rather than letting the objection's
third clause stand as an open question.

Our answer is to concede it and narrow the claim. We assert \eqref{eq:eh},
which is a one-element subset check on committed source. We assert the scoped
enumeration of Section~\ref{sec:fieldset}, which is complete over a closed
finite field set and says so. We do not assert \eqref{eq:uh} and have withdrawn
it. What survives is a modeling observation about one deployed system plus
measurements of what it does, and that is what this paper claims to be.

The measurement in Section~\ref{sec:measurement} does not depend on defeating
the objection. It is a recording of what a deployed system did under a fault,
and it would read the same if $U_h$ were never definable.

\paragraph{Priority is not asserted anywhere.} The general mechanism of
fault-state-dependent observation loss belongs to
\cite{dong2024statedependent}. Section~\ref{sec:containment} states a property
of one system's structure and nothing more. We assert no priority.

\paragraph{Simulation fidelity, and what the campaign did about it.} The
internal fault model produces denial by invalidating the fix flag, and real
GNSS degradation is richer. Section~\ref{sec:qgvalid} addresses this directly
by adding fault classes that keep the fix, and the result is that the gate
does not close for them. The remaining gap is that the ArduPilot receiver
model is still a model: it dropped the fix at four satellites where a real
receiver may hold one, and it reproduces neither gradual carrier-to-noise
decay nor multipath.

\paragraph{Six flights per arm are not six independent observations.} The home
sites do differ, at 216 times the within-site spread, but the injection is
scripted and the simulated receiver is deterministic, so the per-flight closure
rate is identical across flights within each arm. The bootstrap interval is
degenerate and carries no information about precision, at any $n$ we tested. We
report it as such. Wind was intended as a second axis of variation and turned
out to apply no force in this build, so the campaign varied one axis and was
described as varying two.

\paragraph{Replication remains the binding constraint, and the baseline is
named.} Every measurement in this paper is against \textbf{ArduCopter 3.3
SITL} (dronekit-assets prebuilt, 2015-10-22) and the bench simulator. One
deployed-system episode and two simulator campaigns against a single autopilot
build, from 2015, is not a replication base. Every SITL number here should be
repeated on a current ArduPilot and on PX4 before it is relied on, and the
satellite-ladder result in particular is a statement about one simulator's
GNSS model rather than about receivers.

\paragraph{Both mechanisms have been repeated on current stacks, and this paper
deliberately reports neither.} The history-dominated threshold-crossing
mechanism of Section~\ref{sec:algebra} was re-run under a preregistration
frozen before its first trial, on \textbf{ArduPilot 4.8} SITL: twenty valid
trials, the source recursion reproducing the runtime trust trajectory from the
platform's own measured banked state, and the observed threshold crossing
predicted with zero tick error on every ramp. On \textbf{PX4 SIH} that
intervention is \emph{inapplicable} --- no fix-preserving GNSS position offset
exists there, four candidate routes were read out of source and all four fail,
and the preregistration forbids substituting another intervention, so no trials
were run.

The self-occlusion mechanism itself --- the subject of
Section~\ref{sec:measurement} --- has also been repeated, on \textbf{both}
current stacks, and \textbf{at $n=1$ fault trial plus one matched
fix-preserving control per platform}. The gate weight supporting
\code{gps\_fault} collapses to exactly zero when the fix is lost (ArduPilot
$3.0\to0.0$, PX4 $1.0\to0.0$), the diagnosis keeps \code{nominal} on top and
never promotes \code{gps\_fault}, and neither matched control shows any of it.
The intervention was validated by its downstream effect --- the observed loss
of the GNSS position channel with \code{NO\_3D\_FIX}, not the parameter write
--- before any scoring. \textbf{One trial per platform is a demonstration, not
a replication base}, and no rate, frequency or exposure figure may be taken
from it; what it establishes is that the mechanism is not an artifact of a
2015 build.

None of that appears in this paper's tables, and the omission is enforced
rather than promised: this manuscript's baseline is 3.3, mixing two autopilot
builds into one table is the error the project's phase guard exists to prevent,
and it fails the build if a Phase-I artifact references a later phase. The
record is \code{research/v1\_1/CURRENT\_STACK\_REPLICATION.md} with results in
\code{research/v1\_1/results/current\_stack\_replication.json},
\code{mechanism\_a\_ardupilot.json} and \code{mechanism\_a\_px4.json} in the
same directory, and an admissibility audit of the self-occlusion trials in
\code{research/systems-paper/CURRENT\_STACK\_MECH\_A\_EVIDENCE\_AUDIT.md}. A
referee who wants the current-stack evidence should read it there rather than
infer its absence.

\paragraph{No operational harm is demonstrated, and none is claimed.} The
vehicle completes every scenario in this paper. The failsafe escalates, the
navigation reconfigures, and nothing is lost. We have no evidence that a
misattribution ever cost anything operationally, we do not have the data that
would establish it, and we make no such claim. What we report is that a layer
whose product is a cause distribution reported \code{nominal} at occupancy
$1.0$ for an entire fault window --- a defect in the thing it exists to
produce, not a demonstrated hazard.

\paragraph{The gated naive-Bayes design is one implementation, and we do not
claim it is representative.} A structural-analysis FDI design would not exhibit
this at all: it would mark the fault set unisolable rather than treat an
unavailable residual as absent evidence. Our finding is about one deployed
layer whose gate \emph{skips} a term, which is a natural way to implement ``not
applicable'' and is why we think the design rule of
Section~\ref{sec:rule} is worth stating --- but $n=1$, and nothing here
establishes how common the pattern is. The rule is offered as a check, not as
evidence of prevalence.

\paragraph{The reported scalars are model states, not calibrated
probabilities.} The trust coefficient is a Beta-recursion state under adaptive
forgetting. The diagnosis output is a normalized hypothesis weight: a tempered
softmax over exponentially smoothed feature log-scores. Neither is calibrated, we do not calibrate
them, and no claim of calibration is made anywhere in this paper. We
deliberately call it neither a posterior nor a \emph{confidence}: a leader at
$0.9998$ with no evidence distinguishing it from its nearest rival carries no
information about that pair, and the word invites a reader to do arithmetic
that is not licensed.

\paragraph{The abstaining policy is measured, not validated.} It is research
code scored on three flights per arm against one simulator. Its zero nuisance
cost is a property of those flights, and a policy that abstains whenever any
hypothesis's support is silenced will abstain more often on a platform with
more gates. Nothing in production was changed.

\paragraph{Two of the four exposure quantities are unavailable from
operational data.} $T_{FH}$ and $T_{SOFH}$ both condition on $F_h$, the
ground-truth presence of the fault, which does not exist in unannotated
telemetry. Only $T_{NA}$ and $T_{UH}$ can be estimated from a flight corpus,
and the 26-log corpus used elsewhere in this project carries no health labels.
Any deployment wishing to track false-healthy exposure must either annotate its
corpus or run controlled injection; there is no way to recover $F_h$ after the
fact.

\paragraph{The escalation defect is characterised and not fixed.} Section
\ref{sec:algebra} derives it, predicts the boundary and tests the prediction.
No change was made to the trust recursion, the detector decay or the failsafe
dwell. A correction belongs in a separate authorised change with
before-and-after evidence.

\section{Conclusion}

A deployed aerospace diagnostic layer asserted health for 96\% of a fault
window while the fault destroyed the evidence that would name it, with its
trust channel independently agreeing. The escalation path held, through a
mechanism that does not consult the diagnosis, and bounded the reported
exposure to about a second. The naming failure is not bounded by anything.

The condition is narrower than it first appeared and we found that out by
testing it: only faults that remove the GNSS fix produce it, and when the fix
survives the layer sees the fault and declines to escalate for an unrelated
reason. Neither failure is one of observability. The receiver observables that
detect GNSS denial are well established, and in our own satellite ladder the
fault sat in a telemetry field the receiver reported correctly and the
ingestion path does not read. What we measure is evidence routing and the
absence of a vocabulary for ``I cannot assess this''.

We claim no mathematics. The frameworks were already there; what was missing
was somebody running the measurements on a real system and reporting what came
back, including the parts that took claims away.

What we would tell someone building a gated diagnostic layer, all of it
measured rather than argued:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A hypothesis whose evidence sits entirely behind one gate needs
one relation that gate does not control.} Not more evidence: one ungated
relation. Absorption into the healthy class runs at 1.000 with every feature
behind the gate and 0.000 with exactly one outside, at every signature density
we tested. The check is a linear-time subset test over the signature matrix and
the gate map, and it belongs in the build.
\item \textbf{A saturating detector with a fixed decay cannot escalate on
magnitude.} Once the residual pins at zero, a larger fault buys only
logarithmic extra time on an already-saturated signal. If escalation integrates
evidence, the escalation condition is a duration, and it should be stated and
tested as one.
\item \textbf{Size the escalation delay from the concentration cap, not from
the threshold margin.} In this system the delay is dominated by the belief mass
banked during calm flight: the two terms of $N^*$ are $118.82$ against $1.0$. A
forgetting factor chosen for smoothness silently sets the worst-case time to
alarm.
\item \textbf{A monitor reading raw measurements and an estimator reading
fused output have complementary blind spots, and complementary is not the same
as covering.} Verify them jointly on the same fault grid. Ours are both blind
to slow, moderate errors, and the uncovered region widens as the fault slows.
\item \textbf{Give the system a way to say it cannot assess something.} Absence
of applicable evidence surfaced as health here because there was no other state
to surface it as. Adding one removed the entire self-occlusion exposure at zero
nuisance cost, and did nothing for the fault where evidence was present and
ignored. Two defects, two fixes.
\item \textbf{Separate detection from escalation, and unsupported verdicts from
false ones, before reporting either.} Pooling them attributed our largest
measured exposure to the wrong mechanism, and we only caught it by measuring
applicability separately from ground truth.
\end{enumerate}
