Regular Service

Food moves from expo to a runner, fast.

Each run is offered to one runner at a time. If nobody claims it, it goes to backup, then to the floor lead.

Built for the middle of a shift.

Staff screens put the task that needs attention first. Big tap targets, plain-language labels, and status you can read without relying on color.

●

One owner per handoff

A claimed run stays with its owner until it's delivered, given back, or reassigned by a lead. Two people tapping at once still produces one owner.

→

Backup coverage that runs itself

Offers advance on a timer that runs on the server, not in someone's browser. Close every tab and the run still moves to backup.

✓

Done means physically done

Runs close when the food is on the table. Tables free up when a busser confirms the reset — and a late reset from the last party can't release a newly seated table.

▲

Honest when the connection drops

If a tap doesn't reach the server, the screen says it's unconfirmed and re-checks the floor before anyone retries.

‖

Roles that are actually enforced

Runners can't release courses, servers can't change the floor plan, and nobody sees another location's work. Allergy decisions, refunds and guest recovery stay with authorized staff.

↻

Set up from a browser

Create a location, choose your edition, paste table labels, assign roles, pair phones, and run a practice ticket. No hardware to install.

What's in the first release

Dispatch runs on manual events from expo and the floor. Read-only POS integrations are planned; they'll start in shadow mode and only drive the floor once your site confirms their events mean what service needs. We store only what dispatch needs: no guest names, payment details, payroll, or staff location tracking.
