## H2 RESULT — **NOT SUPPORTED**

> H2 is supported only if at least one locked scenario produces a decision
> conflict where the latent fault requires response A, the current diagnostic
> output supports response B or unsupported confidence, and an independent
> baseline or ground truth establishes A and B materially different.

28 decision conflicts were observed across the locked campaign. **24 of them
are in `armed_gate_pair` condition F, which is physically inconsistent by
construction** — the vehicle reports disarmed while its own sensors show it
flying — and they are excluded, as the preregistration says they must be.

That leaves **4 conflicts in physically consistent conditions**: 3 in
`heading_course` F and 1 in `heading_course` G. Both are `MISSED_ESCALATION` —
the latent mag fault required `CAUTION_GENERIC`, nothing was issued, and those
two responses are in different response classes, so the evaluator does call
them materially different.

**But the matched control produces the same conflict.** F and G run the same
4-second fault on the same trajectory from the same seed; the only difference
is whether the speed gate was shut while the fault ran.

| | fault-window occlusion | missed | discordant |
|---|---|---|---|
| F (gate shut) | 3.99 s of a 4.0 s fault | 3 / 24 | 3 |
| G (gate open) | 0.00 s | 1 / 24 | 1 |

Seed-paired, exact McNemar, two-sided: **p = 0.625**, against a preregistered
α of 0.05. Three-versus-one on 24 paired seeds is what chance looks like.

So the conflicts exist and **none of them is attributable to the occlusion**.
Preregistered rejection criterion (d): unable to produce a decision conflict
attributable to the gate.

### Why `armed_gate_pair` could not produce one at all

| condition | fault-window occlusion | first response | position error |
|---|---|---|---|
| B (disarmed through the ground phase) | **0.00 s** | 31.36 s | 63.574 m |
| C (armed throughout) | **0.00 s** | 31.365 s | 63.574 m |

Identical to three decimal places. The reason is structural and was predicted
in the preregistration before the run: `armed_gate` closes only while the
vehicle is disarmed, and a disarmed vehicle is not flying. The occlusion
window and the window in which decisions are taken **do not overlap at all**.

`FailsafePolicy.update` makes the same point in the frozen core's own code:

```python
if getattr(state, "armed", None) is False:
    self.reset_timers()
    return []
```

The decision layer abstains under exactly the condition that closes the gate.
Preregistered rejection criterion (a): correctly handled by an independent
path — here, the decision layer's own arming gate.

**This is the load-bearing finding of Phase 3, and it is a negative one.** The
occlusion enumerated in Phase 2 for these two gates is structurally real and
operationally unreachable on this airframe.

### The apparatus was checked, and it responds

A campaign that reports "no consequence" from a design where the occlusion
never reaches the decision window has restated its own setup, not tested
anything. `armed_gate_pair` F forces the overlap to 39.00 s and produces
**24/24 `MISSED_ESCALATION`** with position error rising from 63.574 m to
66.048 m. The measurement is not blind; the conditions are.

## H3 RESULT — **NOT SUPPORTED**

> H3 is supported only if the support-aware policy reduces incorrect
> actionable decisions on locked scenarios.

**P0 made zero incorrect actionable decisions on all 216 locked scenarios, so
there was nothing to reduce.** P2 is identical to P0 on every scenario: it
withdrew no response, abstained never, and its total abstention time is
0.0 s.

**P2 was inert.** That is neither a defence of it nor a refutation of it — it
is the absence of a test. The condition P2 exists for, a confident attribution
issued while the evidence that would separate it is gated off, occurred
**0 times** in 216 scenarios (`unsupported_confidence_scenarios = 0` for all
three policies).

**H3 is not a safety guarantee and nothing here supports calling it one.**
Abstention is not a solved safety problem; a policy that abstains moves the
problem to whoever reads the abstention.

**The three policies are indistinguishable on every measured axis.** There is
no tradeoff to report, because neither alternative policy ever fired:

- **P1's freshness rule never changed a response.** The faults injected here
  degrade data without stopping it, so no monitored sensor went stale and the
  strongest reasonable freshness fallback had nothing to act on. Preregistered
  rejection criterion (c) — "resolved by existing freshness logic" — is
  therefore **not** the reason H2 failed: freshness resolved nothing, because
  there was nothing for it to resolve.
- **P2's withdrawal never fired.** An attribution was never issued while the
  pair it discriminates was unidentifiable.

The costs H3 asks to be reported are all **zero**, and they are zero because
the policy was inert, not because it is free.

Ambiguity time of 1152.0 s is identical across policies because it is a
property of the evidence, not of the policy: it counts airborne time in which
`imu0` and `imu1` were not separable. Over 216 scenarios that averages 5.33 s
each, and it changed nobody's decision.

## EVIDENCE LEVEL

**`MODEL_BASED_ACTION_CONSEQUENCE`.** The diagnosis half is not modelled —
`TrustState` comes from the frozen `csia.runtime.online.OnlineTrustLayer`, the
faults from the frozen `csia.sensors` injector, P0's response from the frozen
`csia.runtime.failsafe.FailsafePolicy`, and the LOW-sets from the frozen
`csia.diagnosis.causal.SOURCE_INDICATORS`. What is modelled is the vehicle's
response to the response: `csia_consequence/consequence_model.py`, every
constant declared in the preregistration before the locked partition opened.

**SITL was available and was not used, and not for lack of a binary.** Both
`/home/user/px4/build/px4_sitl_default/bin/px4` and
`/home/user/ardupilot/ardupilot/build/sitl/bin/arducopter` exist in this
container. Reaching `SITL_CLOSED_LOOP_EXECUTION` means arming a simulated
vehicle and commanding it so the autopilot executes CSIA's recommendation,
which the phase's scope clause forbids and which the architecture does not
grant CSIA.

## The assumption every H2 result is conditional on

`WRONG_SOURCE_FACTOR = 1.60` — selecting the faulty inertial unit costs more
than selecting neither. Without it, a confident wrong attribution would be
indistinguishable from silence and H2 could not be falsified in either
direction. It is declared, it is **not measured**, and a reader who rejects it
should read the H2 result as conditional on it. In this campaign it never
bound, because no incorrect attribution was ever issued.

## Mutation: two survivors, and what they are a symptom of

38 mutants, **36 killed, 2 survived, score 0.9474**; unmutated baseline exit
0, canary killed, and all 36 kills attributed to a named test node (no bare
file paths, so no collection errors masquerading as kills). The Phase-3
family alone is 13 evaluated, 11 killed, 0.8462.

The two survivors are `P3-03` (report every edge gate as fully open) and
`P3-04` (ask the global support question instead of the decision pair's).
**No test objects to either.** Following this repository's rule, they are not
patched over with a test written to catch that one mutant. They are recorded
as a finding about the evidence.

And rather than argue about whether they could matter, it was measured: the
development campaign was re-run with each mutation monkeypatched in, and the
independent evaluator's verdicts compared.

| mutant | outcome classes changed | support fields changed |
|---|---|---|
| `P3-03` all gates reported open | **0** | 36 of 288 |
| `P3-04` global support in place of the pair | **0** | **0** |

`P3-03` moves the recorded ambiguity and unsupported-confidence numbers and
changes no verdict. `P3-04` changes **nothing at all** — the global support
state and the decision-pair support state coincided on every development
scenario, so there was nothing for a test to catch.

**Both survivors are a symptom of the same thing H3 reports: P2 never fired.**
When the support-aware path never withdraws a response, nothing downstream can
distinguish real gate weights from all-open ones, and no test can pin them.
The way to kill these two mutants is not a new assertion; it is a scenario in
which support actually changes a decision, which is precisely what this
campaign failed to produce.

## CLAIMS WE CAN MAKE

1. For `accel_motor_current` and `accel_esc_rpm` on this airframe, the
   occlusion enumerated in Phase 2 has **zero overlap** with airborne flight,
   measured at 0.00 s of fault-window occlusion over 48 locked scenarios.
2. Conditions B and C for those gates produce **identical** first-response
   times and position errors to three decimal places.
3. For `heading_course`, the gate is shut for 3.992 s of a 4.0 s fault in a
   **physically consistent** flight regime (armed, airborne, past `arm_time`,
   hovering), and the fault is still detected in 21 of 24 seeds.
4. The seed-paired difference between the gate-shut and gate-open conditions
   is 3/24 versus 1/24 missed, **exact McNemar p = 0.625**, not distinguishable
   from chance at α = 0.05.
5. P0, P1 and P2 are identical on all 216 locked scenarios.
6. The campaign replays byte-identically.
7. The apparatus responds: the forced-occlusion control produces 24/24 missed
   escalations.

## CLAIMS WE CANNOT MAKE

- That any vehicle behaved in any way. **Nothing flew.**
- SITL closed-loop, HIL, hardware, or flight validation of anything.
- That the occlusion is harmless in general. It was tested on **3 of 8**
  Phase-2 candidates, one airframe profile, one trajectory family, two fault
  families, in simulation.
- That P2 is safe, unnecessary, or beneficial. It never fired.
- That freshness logic covers the occlusion. P1 never fired either; that is
  untested here, not confirmed.
- That abstention is a solved safety problem.
- Novelty, certification, airworthiness, or universal prevalence.
- Anything about the five candidates out of scope: `temp_bias_drift` (gate
  constant at 0.2, never closes), `phys_gps`, `phys_mag`, `phys_vibration`
  and `hist_vr_withdrawal` (not exposed by `TrustState`).

## NEXT PHASE

Phase 4 — detector delay/false-alarm curves against a matched CUSUM
comparator, at **matched false-alarm operating points over a preregistered
threshold grid**. The preregistration forbids any other comparison, so a
hand-picked threshold against a hand-picked threshold is not a smaller
Phase 4; it is a violation of it.

Phase 3 removal actions, in the order they would change the most:

1. **A fault family that escalates past `CAUTION`.** Three of the recorded
   fields — time to safe state, energy margin, geofence margin under descent
   — carry no information until one exists.
2. **A read-only accessor on `TrustState` for the `P` node gates and the
   `_hist_ok` withdrawal**, which would bring four more Phase-2 candidates
   into scope. It is a frozen-core change and needs its own authorization.
3. **A trajectory generator that varies ground speed independently of yaw.**
   `hover_circle`'s hover segment contains a 90° yaw sweep, so F-versus-G
   still carries a residual confound that the matched fault duration does not
   remove.
4. **A heading-only mag fault** — a hard-iron style error that moves heading
   without moving field magnitude. The frozen injector has no such kind, so
   the one fault that would maximally stress `heading_course` could not be
   run.
5. A separately authorized SITL campaign, with the arming clause amended, to
   reach `SITL_CLOSED_LOOP_EXECUTION` for the executed half.
