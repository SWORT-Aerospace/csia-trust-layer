# Real-world cases (key question 7)

Closed-loop evidence gap search, 2026-09-28. Source ids `Lnnn` are defined in
`research/closed_loop_evidence_gap/PROBLEM_LANDSCAPE.md` §2.

**The question.** Are there real cases where control, adaptation or
reconfiguration masked or changed evidence in a way that complicated
diagnosis or assurance? These cases would show that the problem matters
outside XSYS-001.

All incident facts below are **V0**, from search summaries of investigation
reports. None of the reports was read in full, and each carries
REQUIRES_EXTERNAL_FULL_TEXT_VERIFICATION. Only what the summaries state is
recorded.

## 1. Cases

| case | the system's own action | how later evidence changed | endogenous to the system's own action? | lesson recorded | source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| China Airlines 006, 1985 | autopilot aileron compensation for asymmetric thrust | the bank angle stayed nominal until the aileron limit; the crew focused on airspeed | **yes: controller masking** | control masked the physical cause until authority ran out | L057 |
| American Eagle 4184, ATR 72, 1994 (and five earlier ATR 42 upsets) | autopilot compensation in icing | the aircraft looked normal until the autopilot disengaged into an upset | **yes: controller masking** | fly manually in icing (NTSB) | L059 |
| Three Mile Island Unit 2, 1979 | the command to close the PORV | the indicator reported the command, not the valve | **yes: evidence generated by the action itself** | indicate actual position | L052 |
| Air France 447, 2009 | crew pitch inputs, through airspeed, changed AoA validity | the stall warning stopped as the stall deepened and returned when the nose was lowered | **yes: action-conditioned validity** | validity gating can invert the meaning of a warning | L053 |
| SOHO, 1998 | ground command sequences during gyro calibration; a ground command turning off a gyro in response to unexpected telemetry | the safe-mode controller and the operators worked from a gyro configuration the commands had changed | **yes: operator and sequence interventions** | configuration and command-sequence control | L054 |
| Malaysia Airlines 124, 2005 | redundancy management switched to the backup accelerometer without reporting the failure | backup-only data carried no record of the earlier failure; its later failure reached the flight controls | **yes: redundancy-management action** | fault reporting and status retention | L055 |
| Qantas 72, 2008 | the validity algorithm's 1.2 s memorisation | values accepted after the hold coincided with a second spike and produced a pitch-down | **yes: validity-handling action** | algorithm redesigned; one case in over 28 million flight hours | L061 |
| Uber ATG, Tempe, 2018 | re-classification of a tracked object | tracking history was discarded, so predicted paths restarted each time | **yes: estimator action** | retain history across re-classification | L056 |
| B-2, Guam, 2008 | air-data calibration with moisture present | the computed AoA and airspeed were wrong at take-off | **partly: a maintenance-time estimator action** | moisture procedure | L060 |
| Hitomi, 2016 | control acting on an over-estimated gyro bias after switching to star-tracker data | control created real momentum from an apparent rotation | **partly: estimator-control loop** | parameter and procedure review | L058 |
| Schiaparelli, 2016 | none by the vehicle; a saturation flag persisted | navigation integrated to a negative altitude | **no: a validity-persistence case, not an endogenous one** | saturation handling | L062 |
| XSYS-001 (simulation) | PX4 rate-loop compensation | the dynamics witness read nominal after compensation: 55/55 wrong when used outside its window | **yes: controller masking** | read each witness only in its window; read the compensation witness later | L069 |

## 2. Assessment

- **The problem matters outside XSYS-001.**
  - Ten of the eleven real cases (excluding the XSYS-001 simulation)
    involve evidence that changed because of the system's own action
    (controller, estimator, redundancy management, operator or crew). Eight
    do so fully; B-2 and Hitomi partly.
  - The consequences included:
    - upsets (L057, L059, L061);
    - loss of control of a spacecraft (L054);
    - loss of the vehicle (L058, L060);
    - fatalities (L053, L056, L059).
- **The cases are old, and each was fixed locally.** Each has a specific
  corrective lesson:
  - fly manually in icing;
  - indicate actual position;
  - retain history;
  - redesign the algorithm;
  - report the fault.
- **None of the lessons, in the summaries, calls for a new theory.** Each is
  an instance of a known principle:
  - feedback masking (L002, L020);
  - action-conditioned validity (L035);
  - integrity after an estimator action (L040);
  - provenance (L043).
- **Direct aerospace evidence exists for the propulsion and estimation forms
  of the phenomenon.** No incident was identified in the searched sources
  for the power, communications or compute forms (`PROBLEM_LANDSCAPE.md`
  §6).
- **Importance is established.** Unsolvedness is not: these cases support
  real-world importance scores, not unsolvedness scores.
